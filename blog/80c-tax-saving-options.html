10 Ways to Save Tax Under Section 80C

Save up to ₹1.5 lakh tax legally with these 80C options.

Section 80C is the most popular tax-saving section in India. It allows you to claim deductions up to ₹1,50,000 per financial year. If you are in the 30% tax bracket, this can save you over ₹46,800 in taxes (including cess).

What is Section 80C?

Section 80C of the Income Tax Act allows deductions for investments and expenses made for specific purposes. Both salaried and self-employed individuals can claim this deduction under the Old Tax Regime.

Note: Section 80C deductions are not available under the New Tax Regime.

1. Public Provident Fund (PPF)

PPF is a government-backed savings scheme with a maturity period of 15 years. It offers tax-free interest and is one of the safest options.

  • Maximum investment: ₹1,50,000 per year
  • Interest rate: ~7.1% (varies quarterly)
  • Tax status: EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt)

2. Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS)

ELSS mutual funds invest in equity markets and offer the shortest lock-in period among 80C options — just 3 years.

  • Maximum investment: ₹1,50,000 per year
  • Returns: Market-linked (12-15% historically)
  • Lock-in: 3 years

3. Life Insurance Premium (LIC)

Premiums paid for life insurance policies qualify for 80C deduction. The policy must be for yourself, spouse, or children.

  • Maximum deduction: ₹1,50,000
  • Condition: Premium should not exceed 10% of sum assured

4. Employee Provident Fund (EPF)

If you are a salaried employee, your contribution to EPF is automatically deducted from your salary and qualifies for 80C.

  • Contribution: 12% of basic salary
  • Employer contribution: Also 12% (not counted in 80C)

5. National Savings Certificate (NSC)

NSC is a government savings scheme with a 5-year lock-in period. Interest is compounded annually but paid at maturity.

  • Interest rate: ~7.7%
  • Lock-in: 5 years

6. Senior Citizen Savings Scheme (SCSS)

SCSS is designed for citizens aged 60 and above. It offers higher interest rates than regular fixed deposits.

  • Interest rate: ~8.2%
  • Maximum investment: ₹30 lakh
  • Tenure: 5 years (extendable by 3)

7. Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY)

SSY is a small savings scheme for a girl child below 10 years. It offers the highest interest rate among all government schemes.

  • Interest rate: ~8.2%
  • Tenure: 21 years
  • Maximum investment: ₹1,50,000 per year

8. Home Loan Principal Repayment

The principal amount repaid on a home loan qualifies for 80C deduction. Interest is claimed separately under Section 24(b).

  • Deduction: Up to ₹1,50,000 for principal
  • Note: Property should not be sold within 5 years

9. Tuition Fees for Children

Tuition fees paid for your children's education (up to 2 children) qualify for 80C deduction.

  • Applicable: Only for full-time education in India
  • Not applicable: Donation, development fees, or coaching

10. National Pension System (NPS)

NPS contributions qualify under both 80C (up to ₹1,50,000) and 80CCD(1B) (additional ₹50,000).

  • Total deduction: Up to ₹2,00,000
  • Lock-in: Until retirement (60 years)

How to Maximize Your 80C Savings

  • Start early: Invest in April to earn interest for the full year
  • Mix options: Combine ELSS, PPF, and EPF for diversification
  • Avoid last-minute: Do not rush in March; plan ahead
  • Track contributions: EPF is automatic; others need action

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Investing only to save tax, not for returns
  • Buying unnecessary insurance policies
  • Forgetting to submit investment proofs to employer
  • Ignoring the New vs Old Regime comparison
