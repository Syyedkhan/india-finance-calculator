10 Ways to Save Tax with Section 80C Investments

Claim up to ₹1.5 lakh in eligible deductions under the Old Tax Regime.

Section 80C is one of the most popular tax-saving provisions in India. It allows eligible taxpayers to claim deductions of up to ₹1,50,000 per financial year under the Old Tax Regime. If you fall in the 30% marginal tax bracket and fully utilise this limit, your tax outgo can reduce by up to ₹46,800 (including 4% cess), subject to applicable rules.

⚠️ Important: ₹1.5 Lakh Is a Combined Limit
The ₹1,50,000 limit is not available separately for each investment. Eligible amounts under Section 80C (along with specified sections like 80CCC and 80CCD(1)) are aggregated and capped at ₹1.5 lakh overall.

Example: If your eligible EPF contribution is ₹80,000 and you invest ₹70,000 in PPF, you have already reached the ₹1,50,000 limit.

What is Section 80C?

Section 80C of the Income Tax Act allows deductions for specific investments and expenses. Both salaried and self-employed individuals can claim this deduction under the Old Tax Regime.

Note: Most 80C deductions are available only under the Old Tax Regime. Certain other provisions, such as eligible employer NPS contributions under Section 80CCD(2), are treated separately and may be available under the New Regime as well.

1. Public Provident Fund (PPF)

PPF is a government-backed savings scheme with a maturity period of 15 years. It is one of the safest long-term savings options.

  • Maximum eligible contribution: ₹1,50,000 per year
  • Interest rate: Government-notified rate, revised quarterly
  • Tax status: EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt)
  • Maturity: 15 years

2. Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS)

ELSS mutual funds invest in equity markets and offer the shortest lock-in period among 80C options.

  • Maximum eligible investment: Part of the combined ₹1,50,000 limit
  • Returns: Market-linked; returns are not guaranteed
  • Lock-in: 3 years

3. Life Insurance Premium

Eligible life insurance premiums paid for yourself, your spouse, or your children can qualify for Section 80C deduction, subject to conditions prescribed under the Income Tax Act.

  • Deduction: Part of the combined ₹1,50,000 limit
  • Condition: Policy and premium must meet prescribed eligibility conditions

4. Employee Provident Fund (EPF)

If you are a salaried employee, your contribution to EPF is deducted from your salary and forms part of your eligible 80C deduction.

  • Employee contribution: 12% of basic salary (eligible under 80C, subject to limits)
  • Employer contribution: Also 12%, but treated under separate tax provisions — not added to your 80C deduction

5. National Savings Certificate (NSC)

NSC is a government savings scheme with a fixed lock-in period.

  • Interest rate: Government-notified rate, subject to revision
  • Lock-in: 5 years

6. Senior Citizen Savings Scheme (SCSS)

SCSS is generally available to individuals aged 60 years or above, with certain eligibility provisions for eligible retirees below 60 under specified conditions.

  • Interest rate: Government-notified rate, revised quarterly
  • Maximum investment: ₹30 lakh (subject to applicable rules)
  • Tenure: 5 years (extendable by 3 years)

7. Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY)

SSY is a small savings scheme for a girl child below 10 years of age.

  • Interest rate: Government-notified rate, revised periodically
  • Maximum annual contribution: ₹1,50,000
  • Maturity: Generally 21 years from account opening

8. Home Loan Principal Repayment

The principal amount repaid on a home loan can qualify for 80C deduction. Interest is claimed separately under Section 24(b).

  • Deduction: Part of the combined ₹1,50,000 limit
  • Important: Certain conditions apply if the property is transferred or sold before the prescribed 5-year period; the earlier deduction may be affected under the tax rules.

9. Tuition Fees for Children

Eligible tuition fees paid for your children's education can qualify for 80C deduction, subject to conditions.

  • Applicable: Full-time education at an eligible institution in India
  • Maximum: For up to two children
  • Note: Admission fees, development fees, donations, and coaching fees generally do not qualify as tuition fees.

10. National Pension System (NPS)

NPS is treated under specific provisions, not as a standard 80C item. Eligible own contributions may qualify under Section 80CCD(1), which forms part of the overall ₹1.5 lakh combined limit. Additionally, up to ₹50,000 may be available under Section 80CCD(1B).

  • 80CCD(1): Forms part of the ₹1.5 lakh combined limit
  • 80CCD(1B): Additional ₹50,000 deduction (over and above the ₹1.5 lakh limit)
  • 80CCD(2): Employer contribution, treated separately (may be available under the New Regime as well)

How to Maximise Your 80C Savings

  • Start early: Plan your tax-saving investments early in the financial year instead of waiting until March.
  • Mix options: Combine ELSS, PPF, and EPF for diversification.
  • Track contributions: EPF is automatic; others require action.
  • Review annually: Tax rules and limits may change every year.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Investing only to save tax, not for returns
  • Buying unnecessary insurance policies
  • Forgetting to submit investment proofs to your employer
  • Ignoring the Old vs New Regime comparison
  • Assuming ₹1.5 lakh is available separately for each investment
