Old vs New Tax Regime FY 2025-26: Which is Better?

Compare both regimes and find out which one saves you more tax.

The Indian Income Tax Department offers two tax regimes — the Old Regime and the New Regime. Choosing the wrong one can cost you thousands of rupees every year. This guide will help you understand both and pick the right one for FY 2025-26.

What is the Old Tax Regime?

The Old Regime allows you to claim various deductions and exemptions such as:

  • Section 80C — Up to ₹1,50,000 (PPF, ELSS, LIC, EPF, etc.)
  • Section 80D — Health insurance premium (up to ₹25,000)
  • Section 24(b) — Home loan interest (up to ₹2,00,000)
  • Section 80CCD(1B) — NPS contribution (up to ₹50,000)
  • HRA — House Rent Allowance exemption
  • Standard Deduction — ₹50,000

What is the New Tax Regime?

The New Regime offers lower tax rates but does not allow most deductions. However, it still provides:

  • Standard Deduction — ₹75,000 (increased in Budget 2024)
  • Rebate under Section 87A — Income up to ₹7,00,000 is tax-free
  • NPS employer contribution — Up to 14% of salary

Old Regime Tax Slabs (FY 2025-26)

  • Up to ₹2,50,000 — Nil
  • ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000 — 5%
  • ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000 — 20%
  • Above ₹10,00,000 — 30%

New Regime Tax Slabs (FY 2025-26)

  • Up to ₹3,00,000 — Nil
  • ₹3,00,001 to ₹7,00,000 — 5%
  • ₹7,00,001 to ₹10,00,000 — 10%
  • ₹10,00,001 to ₹12,00,000 — 15%
  • ₹12,00,001 to ₹15,00,000 — 20%
  • Above ₹15,00,000 — 30%

Which Regime Should You Choose?

The answer depends on your income and deductions:

Choose Old Regime if:

  • Your total deductions exceed ₹3,75,000 (80C + 80D + 24b + HRA)
  • You have a home loan with significant interest
  • You invest heavily in tax-saving instruments
  • Your income is below ₹5,00,000

Choose New Regime if:

  • Your total deductions are below ₹3,75,000
  • You do not have a home loan
  • You prefer simple tax filing without paperwork
  • Your income is between ₹7,00,000 and ₹15,00,000

Example Calculation

Scenario: Annual income ₹12,00,000, deductions ₹2,00,000 (80C + 80D).

Old Regime:

  • Taxable income: ₹12,00,000 - ₹2,00,000 - ₹50,000 (SD) = ₹9,50,000
  • Tax: ₹12,500 + ₹90,000 = ₹1,02,500
  • Cess (4%): ₹4,100
  • Total Tax: ₹1,06,600

New Regime:

  • Taxable income: ₹12,00,000 - ₹75,000 (SD) = ₹11,25,000
  • Tax: ₹20,000 + ₹30,000 + ₹3,750 = ₹53,750
  • Cess (4%): ₹2,150
  • Total Tax: ₹55,900

Result: New Regime saves ₹50,700 in this case.

How to Switch Regimes

Salaried employees can switch between regimes every year at the time of filing ITR. However, business owners can switch only once in their lifetime.
