Salary Structure Guide: From CTC to In-Hand Salary

Understand every component of your salary slip and calculate your real take-home pay.

When you receive a job offer, you are often told a "CTC" (Cost to Company) figure. But the amount that actually reaches your bank account each month is much lower. This guide will explain every component of your salary structure and help you calculate your real in-hand salary.

What is CTC?

CTC stands for Cost to Company. It is the total amount a company spends on an employee in a year, including salary, benefits, and contributions. CTC is NOT your in-hand salary.

What is In-Hand Salary?

In-hand salary (also called take-home salary or net salary) is the amount credited to your bank account every month after all deductions.

Main Components of Salary

1. Basic Salary

Basic salary is the core of your salary structure, usually 40-50% of CTC. It is fully taxable.

  • Forms the base for PF, HRA, and gratuity calculations
  • Higher basic means higher PF and gratuity benefits

2. HRA (House Rent Allowance)

HRA is provided to meet rental expenses. It is usually 40-50% of basic salary.

  • Tax exemption: Available if you live in a rented house
  • Exemption is minimum of: Actual HRA, Rent paid minus 10% of basic, or 50% of basic (metro)

3. Special Allowance

This is a flexible allowance provided to balance the salary structure. It is fully taxable.

  • Usually 20-30% of CTC
  • No specific tax exemption

4. LTA (Leave Travel Allowance)

LTA covers travel expenses within India for you and your family.

  • Exempt up to actual travel cost (twice in 4 years)
  • Only domestic travel qualifies

5. Other Allowances

  • Transport Allowance: ₹1,600/month exempt (₹3,200 for disabled)
  • Food Allowance: Taxable
  • Mobile/Internet Reimbursement: Taxable if no bill submitted

Employer Contributions

1. Employer PF Contribution

Your employer contributes 12% of basic salary to your EPF account. This is part of CTC but not part of your monthly salary.

2. Gratuity

Employer provides gratuity (typically 4.81% of basic). Payable after 5 years of service.

3. Health Insurance Premium

Employer-paid health insurance is part of CTC but not credited to your account.

Deductions from Gross Salary

1. Employee PF Contribution

  • 12% of basic salary
  • Deducted from your monthly salary
  • Goes to your EPF account

2. Professional Tax

  • State government tax, usually ₹200/month
  • Varies by state

3. Income Tax (TDS)

  • Calculated based on your annual income and regime choice
  • Deducted monthly as TDS

4. Other Deductions

  • Group insurance premium
  • Loan EMI (if any)
  • Employee contributions to company schemes

Example: CTC to In-Hand Calculation

CTC: ₹12,00,000 per year (₹1,00,000 per month)

Salary Structure:

  • Basic Salary: ₹30,000/month (36% of CTC)
  • HRA: ₹12,000/month (40% of basic)
  • Special Allowance: ₹25,000/month
  • LTA: ₹2,000/month
  • Transport: ₹1,600/month
  • Employer PF: ₹3,600/month
  • Other Benefits: ₹25,800/month

Gross Salary: ₹70,600/month

Deductions:

  • Employee PF: ₹3,600
  • Professional Tax: ₹200
  • Income Tax (approx): ₹8,000
  • Other: ₹500

Total Deductions: ₹12,300

In-Hand Salary: ₹58,300/month

Why CTC and In-Hand Salary Differ

  • Employer Contributions: PF and gratuity are part of CTC but not monthly salary
  • Taxes: TDS reduces take-home amount
  • Benefits: Insurance, food, and other perks are part of CTC but not cash
  • Retirement Savings: PF is deducted but goes into your retirement fund

Tips to Increase Your In-Hand Salary

  • Restructure Allowances: Increase HRA, LTA, transport (tax-exempt components)
  • Choose New Tax Regime: If deductions are low, new regime reduces tax
  • Submit Proofs on Time: Investment declarations reduce TDS
  • Negotiate Non-Taxable Perks: Company car, meal cards, etc.
  • Use NPS: Additional ₹50,000 deduction under 80CCD(1B)

Common Mistakes

  • Comparing CTC across companies without understanding structure
  • Not checking if employer PF is included in CTC
  • Forgetting to claim HRA exemption
  • Ignoring professional tax in budgeting
  • Not reviewing salary structure annually
