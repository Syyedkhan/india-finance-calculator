Salary Structure Guide: From CTC to In-Hand Salary

Understand every component of your salary slip and calculate your real take-home pay.

When you receive a job offer, you are often told a "CTC" (Cost to Company) figure. But the amount that actually reaches your bank account each month is different. This guide explains every component of your salary structure and helps you calculate your real in-hand salary.

What is CTC?

CTC stands for Cost to Company. It is the annual cost a company attributes to employing you. Its components vary by employer and may include salary, employer contributions, benefits, and variable pay.

Note: CTC is not your in-hand salary.

What is In-Hand Salary?

In-hand salary (also called take-home salary or net salary) is the amount credited to your bank account every month after all deductions.

Main Components of Salary

1. Basic Salary

Basic salary is the fixed core component of salary and is often used to calculate PF, gratuity, and certain allowances. It is fully taxable.

Note: The percentage of basic salary in CTC varies by company and is not a fixed rule.

2. HRA (House Rent Allowance)

HRA is an allowance provided to meet rental expenses. It is usually 40-50% of basic salary, but this depends on employer policy.

  • Tax exemption: Generally available under the Old Tax Regime, subject to conditions
  • Exemption is minimum of: Actual HRA, Rent paid minus 10% of basic, or 50% of basic (metro cities), 40% of basic (non-metro)
  • Note: HRA exemption is not available under the New Tax Regime

3. Special Allowance

Special allowance is generally taxable unless a specific exemption applies.

4. LTA (Leave Travel Allowance)

LTA covers travel expenses within India for you and your family.

  • Exemption: Available subject to prescribed conditions and the applicable block period under the Old Tax Regime
  • Note: Normal LTA exemption is not available under the New Tax Regime

5. Other Allowances

  • Meal benefits and food allowances: Taxed according to the applicable rules and conditions
  • Mobile/Internet reimbursements: May receive different tax treatment depending on the nature of the reimbursement and supporting documentation
  • Transport allowance: Specific exemptions may apply in certain cases (such as for disabled employees); general exemption limits have changed over time

Employer Contributions

1. Employer PF Contribution

Your employer generally contributes 12% of applicable PF wages to your EPF account, subject to EPF rules and the applicable wage ceiling. This is part of CTC but not part of your monthly cash salary.

2. Gratuity

Gratuity may be included as a CTC component based on an employer's calculation method. The actual gratuity payable is determined under the applicable gratuity law and eligibility conditions.

Note: Gratuity eligibility has statutory rules and exceptions; it is not simply "after 5 years" in all cases.

3. Health Insurance Premium

Employer-paid health insurance is part of CTC but not credited to your account.

Deductions from Gross Salary

1. Employee PF Contribution

  • Generally 12% of applicable PF wages, subject to EPF rules and the employer's salary structure
  • Deducted from your monthly salary
  • Goes to your EPF account

2. Professional Tax

  • Applicable only in states/UTs that levy it
  • Rates are determined by the relevant state law
  • The amount is not a universal ₹200/month

3. Income Tax (TDS)

  • Calculated based on your annual taxable income and regime choice
  • Deducted monthly as TDS
  • Depends on salary structure, deductions, exemptions, rebates, and special-rate income

4. Other Deductions

  • Group insurance premium
  • Loan EMI (if any)
  • Employee contributions to company schemes

Example: CTC to In-Hand Calculation

CTC: ₹12,00,000 per year

Illustrative salary structure (varies by employer):

  • Basic Salary: ₹30,000/month
  • HRA: ₹12,000/month
  • Special Allowance: ₹25,000/month
  • LTA: ₹2,000/month
  • Employer PF: ₹3,600/month (subject to EPF rules)
  • Other employer benefits/contributions: ₹27,400/month

How to calculate in-hand salary:

  1. Add up cash salary components (Basic + HRA + Special + LTA + other cash allowances)
  2. Subtract employee deductions (Employee PF, Professional Tax if applicable, other deductions)
  3. Subtract Income Tax TDS (calculated based on taxable income and regime choice)
⚠️ Important: The exact in-hand salary depends on your actual salary structure, applicable deductions, and tax regime. The components shown above are illustrative and vary by employer.

Why CTC and In-Hand Salary Differ

  • Employer contributions: PF, gratuity, and insurance are part of CTC but not monthly cash salary
  • Taxes: TDS reduces take-home amount
  • Benefits: Insurance, food, and other perks are part of CTC but not cash
  • Retirement savings: PF is deducted but goes into your retirement fund

Tips to Improve Your In-Hand Salary

  • Understand your salary structure: Some components may have specific tax treatment under the applicable regime
  • Compare both tax regimes: The better regime depends on your taxable income, eligible deductions, and salary structure
  • Submit proofs on time: Investment declarations can reduce TDS
  • Use NPS (Old Regime): Eligible taxpayers under the Old Regime may claim an additional deduction of up to ₹50,000 under Section 80CCD(1B), subject to conditions

Common Mistakes

  • Comparing CTC across companies without understanding structure
  • Assuming a fixed percentage for basic salary across all employers
  • Forgetting that professional tax applies only in certain states
  • Assuming a fixed TDS amount without calculating actual tax
  • Not reviewing salary structure annually
